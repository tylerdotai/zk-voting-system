\section{Problem}

On-chain DAO voting reveals everything: who voted, what they voted, and when. This violates parliamentary privacy norms and discourages honest voting on controversial motions. Members may vote strategically or avoid voting entirely to stay anonymous.

Current solutions require either:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Centralized identity providers (ENS, Proof of Humanity), which exclude many legitimate voters.
    \item Off-chain voting with on-chain settlement, which adds complexity and trust assumptions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Solution: Two-Phase Architecture}

\subsection{Phase 1: Rob's Rules On-Chain (Now)}

Full parliamentary voting using Robert's Rules of Order. Chair-managed voter allowlist on Sepolia. No ZK, no identity requirements. Pure smart contract governance.

\textbf{Why start here:} Get real governance running on-chain before adding complexity. Rob's Rules itself is sophisticated. It handles amendments, reconsiderations, division calls, and quorum-oriented governance behavior that most simple voting systems skip.

\subsection{Phase 2: ZK Privacy Layer (Future)}

Replace direct \texttt{castVote} with a ZK proof that proves:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The voter is on the eligibility allowlist, without revealing which voter.
    \item The vote choice is valid (yes, no, or abstain).
    \item The nullifier is unique, preventing double-voting.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{On-chain result:} The contract only sees that a valid proof was cast for a proposal. It does not learn identity, vote content, or a link between voter and vote.

\textbf{Circuit:} \texttt{vote.circom} already exists, but needs a circom toolchain fix before zkey regeneration.

\section{Rob's Rules Implementation}

\subsection{Parliamentary Features}

\begin{tabular}{|p{0.31\textwidth}|p{0.61\textwidth}|}
\hline
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{Implementation} \\ \hline
Motion (proposal creation) & \texttt{createProposal()} --- any eligible voter \\ \hline
Seconding requirement & \texttt{secondProposal()} --- another voter must second, reducing spam \\ \hline
Amendments & \texttt{submitAmendment()} + \texttt{approveAmendment()} --- any voter proposes, chair approves \\ \hline
Debate period & Voting opens after seconding and amendment resolution \\ \hline
Recorded vote (division) & \texttt{callForDivision()} --- any member can demand a recorded tally \\ \hline
Reconsideration & \texttt{reconsider()} --- member on the prevailing side can reopen \\ \hline
Majority rule & \texttt{yes > no} means passed; abstains do not count \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\subsection{State Machine}

\begin{lstlisting}
Created -> Seconded -> [Amendments] -> Voting -> Passed/Failed
                            ^______________|
                         (reconsideration)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Why Rob's Rules?}

Robert's Rules of Order is the standard for deliberative bodies. It handles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quorum:} minimum participation requirements
    \item \textbf{Seconding:} protection against spam motions
    \item \textbf{Amendments:} constructive modification before voting
    \item \textbf{Division:} recorded vote on demand
    \item \textbf{Reconsideration:} correction of mistakes without a full reproposal
\end{itemize}

Most blockchain voting systems skip all of this. This system implements the parliamentary process directly.

\section{ZK Circuit Design}

\textbf{Circuit:} \texttt{vote.circom} (Phase 2)

\subsection*{Private Inputs}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{nullifier\_hash} --- prevents double-voting without exposing identity
    \item \texttt{commitment} --- voter identity commitment from the allowlist Merkle tree
    \item \texttt{vote\_choice} --- 0, 1, or 2 for yes, no, or abstain
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Public Inputs}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{merkle\_root} --- proves allowlist membership without revealing which voter
    \item \texttt{proposal\_id} --- binds the proof to a specific proposal
    \item \texttt{nullifier\_hash\_out} --- public record of the spent nullifier
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Verification:} Groth16 proof verified on-chain by \texttt{Groth16VerifierV2}.

\section{Economic Model}

\textbf{Phase 1:} Free to use. Gas costs are paid by voters. The chair pays for proposal creation.

\textbf{Phase 2:} ZK proof generation is computationally expensive. Planned mitigations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pre-compute proofs client-side, with no server round-trip.
    \item Use a bouncer or relayer for optional gasless submission.
    \item Deploy on Layer 2, such as Base, for lower gas costs.
\end{itemize}
